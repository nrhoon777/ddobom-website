ABOUT DDOBOM

Ddobom means “spring again”

Korea’s first non-profit for young adults with cancer
In Korean, bom is both “spring” and “to see.” Ddobom is spring returning — and seeing you again.

🩺 WHY DDOBOM

Cancer does not skip the young

In 2023, 20,253 people in their 20s and 30s were newly diagnosed with cancer in Korea — 55 every day.
Yet almost nothing existed for those who met cancer at the age they were meant to be starting out.

  • 🧬
    “It must run in the family”

    For people in their 20s and 30s, cancer far more often traces to environment — stress, diet, how they live — than to genes.

  • 💪
    “They must have been frail”

    It arrives at the healthiest age too. It is nobody’s fault.

  • 🤫
    So they hide it

    Only at the hospital bed do visitors say quietly: “Actually, I had it too.”

Treatment ends, but life does not simply resume.
That is the time Ddobom exists to protect.

🔍 RETHINK

All of us carried
assumptions about cancer

over90%

Most young adults with cancer have no family history.

It comes from environmental factors — stress, diet, the conditions of daily life — not inheritance. Not “something in the family,” not “a weak constitution.” Which means it can happen to anyone.

Analysis by a major university hospital in Korea

🎖️ A change of perspective

In Korea, many employers give credit for completing military service. So why not for surviving something harder? Coming through cancer is not a liability — it is resilience already proven.

📣 Saying it out loud

We want a society where people can name their cancer without flinching. Attitude alone will not get us there, so Ddobom pushes for policy change — and in the meantime, builds places where people can simply enjoy themselves.

🌱 OUR STORY

Started by someone
who had been there

“Why is there nothing for us?”
Ddobom began with that one question.
2015

🏥 Stage 4 lymphoma,
clear in five months

Lee Jung-hoon was diagnosed with stage 4 Burkitt lymphoma. A young body spreads disease fast — but it answers treatment just as fast. Five months after a terminal diagnosis, he was back in ordinary life.

What he learned

✈️ The body was
the easier part

“Why me?” Self-denial was the real wall. What pulled him over it was a plan: “when this is done, I’m going there.” The will to recover needs something to hold on to.

2016

☕ Passing it on

Visitors kept saying, quietly, “Actually, I had it too.” That is how much people were hiding. He gathered others who felt the same, and that community became a registered non-profit in 2020.

“Young adults with cancer are an iceberg.
What shows above the water is a fraction —
far more are hidden beneath it.”

Lee Jung-hoon · Chairperson, Ddobom

🎯 OUR MISSION

Four directions

So that ordinary life can bloom again.

Changing perceptions

We break down the stigma that follows a cancer diagnosis.

Emotional support

We connect peers walking the same road, so no one walks it alone.

Better care

We speak up for treatment environments and policy that fit young patients.

Returning to life

We walk with people through the long road back to work and daily life.

  1. Recovery, emotional support and advocacy for young adults with cancer
  2. Improving treatment environments and public perception, and proposing policy
  3. Publishing, education and outreach on prevention, treatment and recovery
  4. Other activities necessary to fulfil this purpose
