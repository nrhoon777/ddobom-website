ABOUT DDOBOM

Ddobom means “spring again”

Korea’s first non-profit for young adults with cancer
In Korean, bom is both “spring” and “to see.” Ddobom is spring returning — and seeing you again.

🩺 WHY DDOBOM

Cancer does not skip the young

In 2023, 20,253 people in their 20s and 30s were newly diagnosed with cancer in Korea — 55 every day.
Yet almost nothing existed for those who met cancer at the age they were meant to be starting out.

  • 😕
    They were alone

    No one their age in the waiting room, or in any support group.

  • 💬
    No one to talk to

    School, work, dating, fertility — questions only young patients ask went unanswered.

  • 🚪
    Nowhere to return

    Treatment ends, but the road back to ordinary life stays narrow.

Treatment ends, but life does not simply resume.
That is the time Ddobom exists to protect.

🌱 OUR STORY

Started by someone
who had been there

“Why is there nothing for us?”
Ddobom began with that one question.
2015

🏥 Stage 4, at twenty-nine

Lee Jung-hoon, who would go on to found Ddobom, was diagnosed with stage 4 Burkitt lymphoma. He made it back — and found that nothing existed for young survivors, and no one was challenging the stigma.

2016

☕ A small circle

He opened a community with others who felt the same. Travel projects and skill-sharing meetups became the place where people first heard: it wasn’t just me.

2020

🌷 A registered non-profit

In August, the group was incorporated as the Korea Youth Cancer Association (Ddobom) — the first organisation of its kind in Korea and designated a tax-deductible donation organization. We work with young adults across all cancer types.

🌸 THE NAME

Ddobom = spring, again

Like spring returning
to those who came through the coldest winter.

I SEE U AGAIN

🎯 OUR MISSION

Four directions

So that ordinary life can bloom again.

Changing perceptions

We break down the stigma that follows a cancer diagnosis.

Emotional support

We connect peers walking the same road, so no one walks it alone.

Better care

We speak up for treatment environments and policy that fit young patients.

Returning to life

We walk with people through the long road back to work and daily life.

🎯 Purpose (Articles of Association, Art. 4)

We carry out the following activities.

  1. Recovery, emotional support and advocacy for young adults with cancer
  2. Improving treatment environments and public perception, and proposing policy
  3. Publishing, education and outreach on prevention, treatment and recovery
  4. Other activities necessary to fulfil this purpose
